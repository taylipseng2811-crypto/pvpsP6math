Step 1 — Foundation

The 5 Key Angle Rules

Almost every geometry problem in PSLE uses these 5 rules. Learn them well and you can solve anything!

① Angles on a Straight Line = 180°

All the angles sitting on top of a straight line always add up to 180°.

📏
Rule: Angles on a straight line add up to 180°
If a = 60° and b = 70°, then c = 180° − 60° − 70° = 50°
a = 70° + b = 110° = 180°

② Angles at a Point = 360°

All angles meeting at a single point form a full turn — 360°.

🔄
Rule: All angles around a point = 360°
Think of a full spin — one complete turn is always 360°.

③ Vertically Opposite Angles are Equal

When two straight lines cross, the angles across from each other are always equal.

✕
Rule: Vertically opposite angles are equal.
If one angle is 55°, the angle directly opposite is also 55°.
Look for an "X" shape — the two angles across the X are always equal!

④ Angles in a Triangle = 180°

The three angles inside any triangle always add up to exactly 180°.

🔺
Rule: Angles in a triangle = 180°
If two angles are 60° and 80°, the third = 180° − 60° − 80° = 40°

⑤ Angles in a Quadrilateral = 360°

Any 4-sided shape (square, rectangle, parallelogram…) has angles that add up to 360°.

⬛
Rule: Angles in a quadrilateral = 360°
Three angles 90° + 90° + 110° → fourth = 360° − 290° = 70°
Quadrilateral = 2 × triangle. So 2 × 180° = 360°. Easy to remember!

📝 Worked Example

Two angles on a straight line are 65° and x°. Find x.
1
Rule: Angles on a straight line = 180°
2
Write: 65 + x = 180
3
x = 180 − 65 = 115° ✅
In a triangle, two angles are 48° and 76°. Find the third angle.
1
Rule: Angles in a triangle = 180°
2
48 + 76 + third = 180
3
124 + third = 180 → third = 180 − 124 = 56° ✅

📝 Finding Angles in Triangles

An isosceles triangle has one angle of 40° at the top. Find the base angles.
1
Isosceles triangle → base angles are equal
2
Let each base angle = b. Then: 40 + b + b = 180
3
40 + 2b = 180 → 2b = 140 → b = 70° ✅
A right-angled triangle has one angle of 35°. Find the third angle.
1
Right-angled → one angle is already 90°
2
90 + 35 + third = 180
3
125 + third = 180 → third = 55° ✅

📝 Worked Example

A parallelogram has one angle of 65°. Find the other three angles.
1
Parallelogram: opposite angles are equal
2
Co-interior angles (same side) add up to 180°
3
Angle opposite to 65° = 65°
4
Other two angles = 180° − 65° = 115° each
In a parallelogram: opposite angles are equal, and adjacent angles add up to 180°.
Step 4 — Parallel Lines

Angles in Parallel Lines

When a line (called a transversal) cuts across two parallel lines, it creates special angle pairs. These are very common in P6 exams!

📝 Worked Example

AB and CD are parallel lines. A transversal crosses them. One angle formed is 55°. Find the alternate angle and the co-interior angle.
1
Alternate angles are equal → alternate angle = 55°
2
Co-interior angles add up to 180° → co-interior = 180° − 55° = 125°
Alternate angles look like the letter Z (or N). Co-interior angles look like the letter C (or U).

⚠️ Common Exam Mistakes

!
Using 180° for quadrilateral angles — remember it's 360° for 4-sided shapes!
!
Forgetting that isosceles triangles have two equal base angles — always check if two sides are equal!
!
Confusing alternate (Z, equal) and co-interior (C, adds to 180°) angles in parallel lines
!
Not stating the property used in working — always write the rule (e.g. "angles on straight line = 180°")
